# Reference-call protocol for the human caller

This is for the person on the investing team who makes the calls. **The agent never contacts referees, customers or founders.** It drafts the plan, the answer bands and the question sheet, and afterwards it writes up the notes or transcripts the team supplies (`skafld-vc:founder-research`, synthesis mode).

What the protocol can and cannot do: no study tests whether founder reference checks predict venture outcomes. In employee selection, reference checks predict job performance at about r = .26 (Schmidt and Hunter, 1998). Treat that figure as a ceiling: the later re-estimate of selection validities found the earlier corrections too generous by .10 to .20 across methods and could not revise the reference-check figure for lack of data, so .26 stands unrevised and is probably high (Sackett, Zhang, Berry and Lievens, 2022). Structure is what gives them that modest validity: highly structured reference reports outperform unstructured ones by two to three times (.21 against .11 observed; Pajo, 1996), and a short structured phone protocol run by untrained staff reached r = .25 (Taylor, Pajo, Cheung and Stringfield, 2004). So the calls use the same questions, behavioural answers and one anchored rating per block. Label the exercise as structured to reduce leniency, not as a predictor.

## 1. Plan the calls

Four referee types, two or three calls each, eight to twelve calls of about 30 minutes (Seraf, n.d.; VCs average about 10 reference calls per closed deal, 8 at early stage, Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan and Strebulaev, 2020):

| Type                          | Who                                                                                                                             | Purpose                                              |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| On-list personal              | Names the founder gave                                                                                                          | Consistency with the founder's own account           |
| Off-list personal             | Former managers, co-founders from prior ventures, direct reports, investors in prior rounds, from the founder-research timeline | Carries more weight than on-list calls               |
| On-list customer              | Customers the company named                                                                                                     | Value, alternatives, renewal intent                  |
| Off-list prospective customer | Buyers in the target segment who are not customers, including at least one churned customer or lost prospect                    | What the market says without the founder's selection |

Rules for choosing referees:

- Professional history only: people who "have actually worked with the person in the past. These people can be past associates, bosses, customers" (GoingVC angel diligence guide, undated).
- Never anyone at a founder's current employer (1752vc, Causo and GoingVC reference-check guides, 2026).
- Personal references before customer references; customer calls last and only when the lead is leaning yes (Seraf, n.d.; Rosenblum, 2022).
- Do not discount short working relationships: referee tenure with the candidate did not affect validity (Taylor et al., 2004).
- Weight answers about competence in the prior role. Discount opinions on "will they be a great founder" unless the referee was the founder or CEO the candidate reported to (Hudson, 2023).

## 2. Write the answer bands before calling

For each hypothesis the team needs confirmed, write down before the first call the answer that would clear the bar and the answer that would not (Rosenblum, 2022). Share the bands with the deal team; for customer calls, share them with the founder too (Rosenblum, 2022). Share the hypotheses, never the question sheet: do not send the questions to the founder or the referees in advance, because a referee who has been briefed on the questions gives rehearsed answers, and identical phrasing across referees is the main coaching tell (reference-check guides, 2026).

```
Hypothesis: <e.g. "The CEO delivers on commitments to customers">
Clears the bar: <a specific, dated example of a commitment kept under pressure>
Does not clear: <only general praise, or an example of a missed commitment with no recovery>
Asked in block: <Reliability>
```

## 3. Question sheet (personal references)

Ask the same questions, in the same order, of every personal referee. Ask for a specific instance each time ("tell me about a time"), not an opinion. After each block, rate it once on the four-point scale below, from what was said, and note the verbatim story that supports the rating.

**Rating scale, per block.** An asymmetric, positively worded scale reduces leniency (Pajo, 1996). Write the rating from the story, not from the referee's tone. The scale's four labels come from Pajo; the behavioural anchors are this protocol's own, since no published anchored scale for founder references exists. Adjust them only before the first call, never between calls.

| Rating      | Anchor                                                                                                                       |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Adequate    | The referee could give no specific example, or the example shows the behaviour only partly                                   |
| Good        | One specific, dated example that shows the behaviour                                                                         |
| Excellent   | Several specific examples, including one under pressure                                                                      |
| Exceptional | Specific examples under pressure that the referee compares favourably with the best people they have worked with, unprompted |

Record "no basis" when the referee did not observe the behaviour; it is not a low rating.

**Block 1. Relationship.** When did you first work with <name>, and in what capacity? How closely did you observe their work? Are you still in contact, and how often? (No rating; this block sets how much weight the rest carries.)

**Block 2. Reliability.** Tell me about a specific commitment <name> made to you or to a customer, and what happened. Did they finish what they started, or move on to the next thing? (Delivering reliably on commitments is the behaviour most associated with CEO success in a large executive-assessment study; Botelho, Powell, Kincaid and Wang, 2017.)

**Block 3. Adversity.** Tell me about a specific time things went badly for <name>. What did they do in the first week? Whom did they hold responsible: themselves, others, or circumstances? How long did it take them to come back with a new approach? Did they get stuck on one approach? (Adapted from the Lipper reference questions in the GoingVC angel diligence guide, undated; treating setbacks as learning rather than failure is associated with CEO success, Botelho et al., 2017.)

**Block 4. Judgement and honesty.** Tell me about a mistake <name> made. How did they talk about it? Can you think of anyone who would question their integrity? Has anyone had a serious disagreement with them, and what was it about? (Lipper questions via GoingVC, undated; 1752vc and Causo guides, 2026.)

**Block 5. People.** How did <name> work with peers, with people who reported to them, and with their own managers? Give me an example of each if you can. Would you work with them again, and in what role? (Lipper questions via GoingVC, undated; reference-check guides, 2026.)

**Overall rating.** On a scale of 1 to 10, how would you rate <name> for the role they are taking now? What would make it a 10? (1752vc, Causo and GoingVC guides, 2026.)

**Coaching detectors.** Did you expect this call? Who else has asked you about <name> recently? Did you tell them the same? (Lipper meta-questions via GoingVC, undated.) After all calls, note shared phrasing across referees: identical vocabulary suggests coaching (reference-check guides, 2026).

**Close.** Is there anything I should know that would only come up if I asked? Who else should I speak to?

**Questions deliberately left out:**

- Anything about family, relationships, health, age or other personal matters (professional history only).
- "Where will they be in ten years?" and similar: these invite opinion, not observation.
- "Would you invest?": it asks for a verdict the referee cannot give (Hudson, 2023).

## 4. Question sheet (customer and prospective-customer calls)

Ask of every customer and prospect (OpenView, 2012; reference-check guides, 2026). As with personal references, the questions are not sent in advance. Include at least one churned customer or lost prospect; call all customers if there are fewer than ten.

1. What were you doing before you used this product?
2. What triggered the search for something new?
3. Which alternatives did you evaluate, and why did you choose this one (or not)?
4. What breaks if it disappears tomorrow?
5. Would it survive a 20% cut in your budget?
6. How has your use or spend changed since you started?
7. Who decided to buy, and how did the purchase go (budget, approvals, implementation)?
8. What is one thing you wish they did better?
9. On a scale of 1 to 10, how likely are you to renew or expand, and what would make it a 10?

Rate the block once: Adequate (no measurable value named), Good (one measurable outcome), Excellent (measurable outcome and expansion or renewal intent), Exceptional (measurable outcome, expansion, and the product is embedded in a process the customer cannot easily replace). "No basis" where the customer has not used it long enough.

## 5. Write-up per referee

```
Referee: <type: on-list / off-list, personal / customer> · relationship and dates · how closely observed
Block ratings: Reliability <rating> · Adversity <rating> · Judgement <rating> · People <rating> (or "no basis")
Verbatim stories: <one per block>
Overall: <1-10>, what would make it a 10: <answer>
Consistency with the founder's own account: match / partial / conflict — <detail>
Against the answer bands: <hypothesis: cleared / not cleared / not tested>
Coaching signals: <none / detail>
```

Roll-up across all calls: the pattern across referees, not any single call; where on-list and off-list referees differ; which hypotheses are cleared, not cleared or untested; and what, if anything, changes in the team assessment.
